# Forge Governance

Forge begins with a lightweight maintainer-led governance model.

## Principles

Governance optimizes for technical coherence, transparent decisions, sustainable maintenance, open contribution, Protocol stability, and low unnecessary bureaucracy.

## Roles

### Lead Maintainer

Has final decision authority when technical consensus cannot reasonably be reached.

### Maintainers

Review contributions, participate in RFCs, maintain project areas, and support releases.

### Contributors

Participate through code, tests, documentation, discussions, issues, RFCs, and Adapters.

## Decision process

Routine implementation decisions may be resolved through Pull Request Review. Material Protocol Changes require RFC. Material internal Architecture Changes require ADR.

Technical consensus is preferred. When consensus cannot be reached, the Lead Maintainer makes the final decision and the rationale should be recorded.

## Protocol stewardship

Protocol changes require greater stability than ordinary implementation changes and should prioritize clarity, interoperability, compatibility, and long-term maintainability.

## Governance evolution

Forge does not initially require a foundation, steering committee, or formal voting system. Governance should grow only when project scale makes additional structure useful.
